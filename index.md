## Project Overview

My capstone project focuses on using artificial intelligence to evaluate the veracity and reliability of news articles.

Our team is developing a veracity engine that uses Veracity Vectors and Factuality Factors to assess the credibility of information presented in news articles.

The project involves applying large language models (LLMs), structured evaluation rubrics, and comparative analysis to identify potential bias, evaluate factual accuracy, and improve transparency in AI-generated assessments.

---

## Quarter 2 Project Plan

### 1. What is the overall goal of the project?

The overall goal of our project is to develop an AI-powered veracity engine capable of evaluating the credibility and factual reliability of news articles.

Our system will analyze articles using Veracity Vectors and Factuality Factors to assess different dimensions of credibility, including source reliability, factual accuracy, and potential bias.

We aim to create a structured evaluation process that produces transparent, explainable assessments rather than relying solely on subjective judgments from large language models.

### 2. What methods will be used?

Our team plans to use several methods to develop and evaluate the veracity engine.

These include:

- **Data Collection:** Gathering news articles from sources representing different political perspectives.
- **Veracity Vectors:** Evaluating articles across multiple dimensions of credibility.
- **Factuality Factors:** Applying structured criteria to assess factual reliability.
- **Large Language Models:** Using Gemini to assist with article analysis and comparative evaluations.
- **Fractal Chain of Thought (FCOT):** Exploring structured reasoning techniques to improve the transparency and consistency of evaluations.
- **Comparative Analysis:** Comparing outputs across different articles, prompting techniques, and evaluation methods.

We will also investigate how clustering and additional training data can improve the system's ability to identify patterns across news articles.

### 3. What data will be used?

Our project will primarily use publicly available news articles collected from online publications.

We plan to gather articles representing a range of political perspectives, including left-leaning, right-leaning, and more neutral sources.

Potential sources include:

- The Guardian
- The New York Times
- PolitiFact
- Other publicly accessible news publications

We will organize the collected articles into a structured dataset for analysis and evaluation.

Where permitted, we may use web scraping or APIs to collect article content.

### 4. How will success be evaluated?

We will evaluate our project's success based on the consistency, reliability, and explainability of the veracity engine's assessments.

Important evaluation criteria include:

- **Consistency:** Whether similar articles receive comparable evaluations under the same rubric.
- **Accuracy:** Whether the engine's assessments align with independently verified factual information.
- **Transparency:** Whether the system clearly explains the reasoning behind each evaluation.
- **Bias Detection:** Whether the engine can identify potentially biased framing across different news sources.
- **Comparative Performance:** Whether different prompting techniques and evaluation methods improve the quality of results.

Our goal is to develop a system that provides meaningful, interpretable assessments of news credibility while minimizing unsupported conclusions.
